# Election Wahala: art direction

## Setting and scope

Build a recognisably Nigerian 3D world through specific places, ordinary activity,
and individual people. The first location is **Ilupeju Community Primary School**,
an invented public primary school in a fictionalised mainland Lagos
neighbourhood, temporarily serving as **Polling Unit 001**. Ilupeju is a real
locality; this school, its street, neighbours, notices, and polling-unit number
are fictional. Do not imply that this layout reproduces an actual school or
official polling station.

This version develops the environment only. The capsule is a temporary player
placeholder, not a proposed character design. Do not add NPCs, crowds, voting
systems, party branding, or political caricatures as part of this pass.

## Environmental language

Use a modest, functioning neighbourhood school rather than an anonymous
"African" backdrop. The compound should read as a place used for teaching on
ordinary days, with polling equipment brought in for an event. Regional identity
comes from the combination of architecture, materials, signage, street context,
and future inhabitants; flags or decorative motifs cannot carry it alone.

- Mix muted sandy soil with practical concrete paths and small, irregular grass
  patches. Avoid a uniformly bright yellow ground or lush lawn covering the
  whole yard. Keep the gate-to-polling-area route easy to see and walk.
- Give the classroom block a shaded veranda, simple painted walls, framed louvre
  windows, and a restrained galvanized-metal roof. Windows need readable depth
  and structure, without expensive transparent panes or dense slat geometry.
- Limit weathering to useful cues: a few lower-wall stains, repaired paint
  patches, uneven concrete tones, and occasional roof discolouration. Include
  maintained walls, fresh repairs, intact furniture, and clear signs alongside
  worn details. Do not make every surface damaged or dirty.
- Keep the voting canopy, registration table, moulded plastic chairs, and ballot
  box recognisable at normal third-person distance. Use shaped seats, slotted
  backs, and splayed plastic legs; introduce armrest variants where appropriate.
- Add a school noticeboard with a few short, fictional everyday notices, such as
  a PTA meeting or library reminder. Main labels must stay legible; do not fill
  the board with illegible pseudo-text. Current notices use simple English.
- Show modest neighbouring buildings beyond the wall with varied but coherent
  heights, paint, roofs, and windows. They establish a lived-in street rather
  than a remote, isolated compound. Reserve detailed modelling for visible faces.
- Include a roadside drainage channel and a broad, level crossing aligned with
  the gate. Preserve passage for the player and camera; drainage should explain
  the street layout without becoming a surprise obstacle on the entrance route.

Use warm daylight, a sky, and sufficient ambient fill for objects beneath roofs
to remain visible. The palette favours dusty beige, grey concrete, subdued
paint, galvanized grey, and restrained greens; small colour accents are welcome.
Avoid applying a sepia filter or exaggerated saturation to imply location.

## People and clothing for later versions

Future voters should represent believable individuals across adult age groups,
genders, skin tones, heights, body builds, hairstyles, occupations, income
circumstances, religious practices, and access needs. Include mobility aids and
accessible circulation when those characters are introduced. Lagos characters
may have different regional backgrounds; a neighbourhood should not become an
ethnic uniform.

Build varied everyday wardrobes: T-shirts, shirts, trousers and jeans, skirts
and dresses, workwear, and contextual ankara or adire garments. Senator sets,
kaftans, modest dress, and head coverings can also appear where appropriate to
the person and occasion. Regional garments are one part of a wardrobe, not a
mandatory costume for every Nigerian character. Mix new, carefully maintained,
and worn clothing without equating wear with a person's worth.

Do not infer political preference, honesty, aggression, wealth, occupation, or
personality from ethnicity, skin tone, religion, or clothing. Give people distinct
silhouettes and personal choices without using physical exaggeration as a joke.
Validate language, names, pronunciation, and culturally specific details with
appropriate references and speakers before using them in dialogue or signs.

## Regional development and technical limits

Each later location needs its own reference set for climate, vegetation,
architecture, infrastructure, languages, and people. A northern, eastern,
riverine, or other setting cannot be created merely by recolouring this Lagos
scene. Avoid treating Nigeria as uniformly desert, rural, affluent, or deprived.

Target Intel Iris Xe graphics, 8 GB RAM, and Godot's Compatibility renderer. Use
shared opaque materials and primitive meshes, sparse repeated detail, simple
static collision, and few shadow-casting lights. Keep decorative grass, stains,
roof ribs, and notice sheets free of unnecessary collision. Solid obstacles
must block the player and camera appropriately, with generous walking routes.

Check the scene in a window at normal play distance: sign readability, muted
colour balance, roof and veranda shade, drainage crossing, camera clearance,
and sustained frame rate. Loading and collision tests do not establish hardware
rendering performance.

## Reference anchors

- [LASIEC electoral wards](https://lasiec.gov.ng/electoral-wards/) establishes
  Ilupeju as a real Lagos locality; it does not identify this fictional school.
- [LASUBEB](https://lasubeb.lg.gov.ng/) provides public-primary-school context,
  including attention to well-organised, maintained facilities. The scene's
  architecture, notice wording, and material treatment remain creative choices.
