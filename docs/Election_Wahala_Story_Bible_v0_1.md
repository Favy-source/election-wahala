## 1. Story authority and creative boundaries

### Confirmed foundations

The player creates their own character. Ordinary Nigerian citizens are the protagonists. Joining a party, taking a campaign role or seeking office is optional. Nigerian people, clothing, environments and everyday experiences must feel specific and believable. The experience includes humour and community warmth alongside election tension, uncertainty, fear, violence and aftermath.

Voter registration and card collection, queues and a fictional bribery encounter belong in the story. Political groups, applications for campaign roles and public office belong in the larger experience. The long-term scope includes all 36 states and the FCT, diaspora Nigerians returning to their registered polling units, and life/transport activities inspired by the appeal of Nigerian life-simulation and danfo games.

The original presidential design allows up to ten player candidates and gives slots to the first ten registrants choosing that path. The exact registration/nomination interaction is unresolved in the master guide. This story bible does not replace it with a different rule.

### Proposed creative choices in this document

The first authored campaign takes place in **Akinwale**, a fictional Lagos neighbourhood. Its community school serves as a polling location. The central local concern is a damaged drainage crossing and access route connecting homes, businesses and the school. Its condition gives residents a concrete reason to question campaign promises without reducing the whole election to one construction project.

The named cast, political groups, repair dispute, specific incidents, personal objectives and endings below are original proposals. This is a detailed narrative treatment with implementable mission structures, not a complete recording script for every incidental NPC line.

Election procedures are represented through the chosen game ruleset. Before shipping, research the represented period and adjust registration, accreditation, counting, staff roles and movement restrictions. Fictional officials and incidents are not claims about named real people or every Nigerian election.

Do not make an ethnic group, religion, language, gender or region a shortcut for a character’s morality. The recurring cast has individual ambitions and contradictions. The serious themes are retained, but full firearm combat and permanent player-character death remain undecided.

## 2. The central storyline

### 2.1 Working season title: The Road Home

Akinwale’s residents share a narrow route past a drainage crossing to the school, bus stop and nearby market. It is familiar enough that people give directions by the shops beside it. It is also unreliable: rain, damage and poor maintenance make it harder to use. Families have improvised ways around the problem while waiting for lasting work.

The approaching election brings promises of improvement, campaign visitors, paid assignments and arguments over who deserves credit. Temporary work appears. Some residents are relieved. Others worry it is a performance that will disappear after voting. Nobody needs to belong to a party to have an opinion about getting home safely or keeping a business open.

Your character enters with a personal goal: establish a stable home and livelihood, or reconnect with a home you left. The same people who help you settle in later need your help. You get your voter’s card, make choices about favours and commitments, and learn how competing versions of events spread.

On election day, the route, the relationships and the information you trust all matter. A problem at the polling area may be administrative, intimidating or violent, depending on the scenario. Your priority can shift from completing a civic intention to finding someone and reaching safety.

After results are announced, residents disagree about what happened and what comes next. A local repair record is incomplete. An unofficial results message conflicts with another account. A shop may have lost stock. Someone may be injured, missing temporarily or dead in a severe story variant. These are distinct problems with distinct evidence; they are not automatically proof of one grand conspiracy.

The campaign ends when you decide what to do with your relationships, responsibilities and knowledge. The repaired or unrepaired road is one visible outcome. The deeper ending is whether you have made a home, broken promises, protected people, pursued power, or chosen to leave.

### 2.2 Player-facing promise

You do not need to save Nigeria or become president to finish the story. You need to live through an election as a person whose decisions matter to other people.

### 2.3 Dramatic questions

- What does a better life mean to your character?
- Who earns your trust, and what happens when someone you trust compromises?
- Can you accept an opportunity without accepting everything attached to it?
- How do you act when the information available is incomplete?
- What do you owe your community when protecting yourself is already difficult?
- When the election ends, which promises will you still keep?

These questions should emerge through tasks and relationships, not lengthy speeches explaining the theme.

## 3. Setting and the neighbourhood’s ordinary life

### 3.1 Akinwale location plan

| Location | Everyday purpose | Story purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Shared residential compound | Home, neighbours, laundry, food, rest | Relationships, preparation and recovery |
| Unity Row | Small shops and work opportunities | Livelihood, campaign promises and social information |
| Bus stop and short route | Travel and driver work | Encounters, fares, diaspora arrival and route choices |
| Registration/collection centre | Separate civic-service location | Queue, paperwork, cooperation and corruption encounter |
| Akinwale Community Primary School | Familiar local landmark | Polling compound and later community meeting |
| Drainage crossing/access route | Practical link between destinations | Repair dispute, navigation and consequence visibility |
| Viewing centre/courtyard | Recreation and conversation | Humour, disagreement and a calm social baseline |
| Clinic exterior/recovery location | Care and contact point | Selected aftermath branches; not a medical simulator |

The school is not automatically the card-issuance centre. Home, constituency and origin are separate character attributes. The player’s registered polling location follows the story ruleset rather than being assigned by whichever building they are nearest to.

### 3.2 Ordinary scenes worth preserving

A neighbour asks whether you have eaten. Someone tries to reserve a chair at the viewing centre. A conductor remembers that you still need change. Shopkeepers compare a good day’s sales. Friends tease one another about over-dressing for an ordinary errand. A household celebrates a small success.

These scenes are not filler. They establish how the neighbourhood sounds and behaves when it is comfortable, making election-day changes perceptible. They also make staying a citizen worthwhile.

### 3.3 Time and scale

The full story spans a fictional campaign period, with time jumps for processes that cannot reasonably complete in a single day. Registration, card readiness and collection are not collapsed into one instant transaction. The exact relationship between story time and online season time is a design decision still to be settled.

The first production slice covers a small route and a few events. The full neighbourhood and all chapters are not required before testing the first mission.

## 4. The player and origin routes

### 4.1 Shared identity

The player chooses appearance, name and a background. The story avoids prescribing gender, ethnicity, party or religion. Dialogue uses the chosen name where practical; voiced lines can use natural forms of address without requiring unlimited name recordings.

The opening asks the player to choose a personal priority: financial independence, family connection or belonging in a new community. This adjusts opportunities and dialogue, not combat statistics or moral worth. No priority excludes the others permanently.

### 4.2 Origin options

| Origin | Opening circumstances | Different early beat | Shared destination |
|---|---|---|---|
| Returning local resident | Coming back after time elsewhere in Nigeria | Familiar people notice what has changed | Establish a present-day place in the community |
| New arrival | Moving in for work or a fresh start | Must learn routes and build trust | Same citizen chapter sequence |
| Diaspora returnee | Returning to family and the election period | Travel, expectations and registration-location checks | Rejoin the local story after arrival |

Production recommendation: build one opening first, then author the others using the same neighbourhood and mission systems. Diaspora is confirmed long-term content, not erased by this implementation order.

### 4.3 The personal through-line

At the beginning, the character needs stability. At the midpoint, an opportunity tests a relationship. On election eve, an unresolved obligation competes with preparation. On election day, someone matters more than an abstract objective. At the end, the player chooses how to live with what happened.

This through-line remains intact for a candidate, driver, shop worker or politically independent resident.

## 5. Recurring characters

Names and biographies are draft and require cultural/dialogue review before final production.

### Bisi Adebayo — the neighbour who makes room for you

Bisi is a skilled tailor working from a modest shop near Unity Row. She has invested in her business and wants a more dependable workspace. She helps the player settle in because someone once helped her, but she is protective of her time and does not exist only to give advice.

Her conflict: a campaign order could stabilise her business, but it comes with expectations about visibility and loyalty. She can accept legitimate paid work without endorsing every action of its organisers. The player may help her clarify that boundary or encourage an arrangement that later becomes uncomfortable.

Arc: practical optimism → a promising contract → pressure over payment or public support → a decision about independence and recovery. Possible aftermath: a continuing business, temporary closure, damaged friendship or renewed partnership. Her distress is not automatically a death scene.

### Chidi Okafor — the friend who needs the next shift

Chidi works as a conductor and hopes to move into more stable driving work. He is observant, funny and quick to read a passenger, but sometimes says yes before considering whether he can keep a promise.

His conflict: helping a campaign transport organiser can pay better than an ordinary shift. Taking the job might leave a family commitment or friend waiting. He is not predetermined to betray anyone.

Arc: familiar travel companion → opportunity and overcommitment → a difficult route decision → reconciliation or distance. He becomes the central separated companion in the default tense election-day branch because the player has spent ordinary time with him first.

### Mariam Bello — the person who keeps asking what is known

Mariam runs a small phone-accessories/printing counter and contributes to a community information channel. She is good at documenting details, but her desire to publish quickly can conflict with careful verification.

Her conflict: an influential contact offers reach and access in exchange for a favourable version of a story. She may resist, hesitate or make an error depending on her authored arc and the player’s interactions.

Arc: helpful information source → pressure to take a side → correction or escalation of a rumour → public accountability. She teaches evidence distinction through her own fallibility, not as an infallible exposition machine.

### Mr. Sanni — the experienced driver

Sanni operates the first danfo route. He recognises regular passengers and notices when the street feels unusual. He values punctuality and income, but refuses to treat passengers as disposable cargo.

His conflict: route reliability, vehicle costs and decisions about when to suspend a journey. He can disagree with the player about a detour without being villainous.

Arc: driver mentor → strained livelihood → election-period route decision → a choice about returning to work or helping recovery. A driver-player can work with him; other players know him as a recurring passenger-route character.

### Efe Dada — the organiser with competing loyalties

Efe coordinates local campaign activity for whichever fictional party is assigned to the scenario. They believe political organisation can achieve practical change and are skilled at finding opportunities for people.

Their conflict: pressure from leadership to show turnout and loyalty, even when community needs suggest a different priority. They may approve an honest paid task while another intermediary makes an improper offer. Do not merge every campaign action into corruption.

Arc: credible invitation → dependence on numbers and appearances → a choice to protect people or protect their position → consequences after the result. The player can become a colleague, critic or independent acquaintance.

### Mrs. Akinola — the collection officer

An experienced fictional civic-service worker responsible for card lookup/issuance in the collection chapter. She can be firm, tired and helpful. She explains real obstacles clearly when the player asks the right question.

Her conflict: an overloaded centre, missing lookup information and rumours that she is refusing people. She is not the default corrupt officer. In the first version, an intermediary exploits confusion around the queue; a separate alternate scene may involve a fictional staff member soliciting payment.

Arc: initially intimidating authority → individual person under pressure → an ally or neutral professional. Her personal life remains modestly suggested rather than making the player solve all her problems.

### Tunde “Chairman” — the unofficial shortcut

Tunde is an intermediary around the service centre. He offers to “help” people move faster, relying on familiarity and uncertainty. He does not represent all workers, conductors, touts or young men.

His conflict is transactional: he wants money and influence. Some promises may be empty. Refusing him must not destroy the player’s legitimate route. Avoid a detailed real-world bribery tutorial; the gameplay concerns trust, pressure and consequences.

### Ngozi Eze — the school caretaker

Ngozi maintains the school environment and knows practical access details. She wants the school usable after polling and resents being treated as invisible when others arrive for a single event.

Arc: background task-giver → valuable source of local knowledge → someone whose work and concerns shape the aftermath. She can provide a safe assembly location in an incident branch, but never a magical solution to every danger.

### Dara — a flexible family contact

Dara is a cousin or close family friend, depending on player origin. Their messages connect the player to life beyond current objectives. They sometimes worry excessively, sometimes have valuable information and sometimes need the player to listen rather than solve something.

No family death is mandatory. In the diaspora route, Dara’s expectations can create tension about what returning means and whether the player will stay.

## 6. Relationships, conflicts and fictional political groups

### 6.1 Relationship model

Track concrete relationship facts: kept promise, unpaid debt, help received, boundary crossed, information corrected and time spent together. Optional broad trust values can support selection, but dialogue should reference specific actions.

A person can trust the player’s reliability while disagreeing politically. Refusing a party membership is not automatically a relationship loss. Helping someone does not create an obligation to vote as they wish.

### 6.2 Fictional groups

Working names for a local offline scenario: **People’s Tomorrow**, **Common Ground Movement** and **Forward Together Party**. These are placeholders, not mappings to real parties. Symbols, colours and names require originality review before release.

Each needs a credible platform, internal disagreement and people with mixed motivations. Do not permanently assign one party “good” and another “evil.” In online play, player-created or selected organisations occupy supported roles once the group-creation rules are settled.

A local organiser’s behaviour can differ from national campaign policy. The story should give the player enough context to understand who made a promise and who can actually act on it. Never imply a president personally controls every pothole or queue.

### 6.3 The repair dispute

The proposed crossing repair is the season’s local thread. Three kinds of record remain separate: residents’ requests, evidence of work actually performed and campaign claims about that work. A missing receipt may indicate an unresolved accounting problem; it does not automatically prove electoral fraud.

The player can help maintain a community record, ask for clarification or ignore the dispute. Completion of the main citizen story does not require becoming an investigative journalist.

## 7. Campaign structure and emotional rhythm

| Chapter | Main question | Dominant feeling | Main relationship focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Before the Posters | Can I build a life here? | Curiosity, warmth, practical worry | Bisi, Chidi, Dara |
| 2. My Name on the Register | What will it take to participate? | Frustration, solidarity, relief | Mrs. Akinola, queue neighbours |
| 3. The Price of Loyalty | What am I agreeing to? | Opportunity, doubt, conflict | Efe, Bisi |
| 4. A Country on the Move | Who can rely on me? | Momentum, humour, strain | Chidi, Sanni, family |
| 5. Election Eve | What should I believe? | Anticipation, uncertainty, brief reassurance | Mariam, Dara |
| 6. Election Day Wahala | How do I get through this with people I care about? | Hope, delay, fear, relief | Chidi and the local cast |
| 7. Who Counted My Vote? | What happened, and what can I actually establish? | Confusion, anger, determination | Mariam, Efe |
| 8. After the Announcement | What kind of life will I continue? | Grief/joy, reflection, resolve | Relationships shaped by play |

Recommended pacing: ordinary scenes earn the later tension. A chapter should include a clear beginning, a playable task, a human complication and a consequence scene. Not every complication is criminal or violent. Severe events belong to deliberate scenario variants and age/content decisions.

## 8. Chapter One — Before the Posters

### Opening sequence

A playable arrival, not a long opening movie. You enter the residential compound with a small bag or return from a short trip. Bisi is trying to move a package out of the walkway. A radio is audible from a room. Someone calls from upstairs about water. A campaign vehicle passes far enough away that its words are incomplete.

Bisi helps orient you after you help move the package or politely explain why you cannot. Dara sends a message asking whether you arrived. The first objective is personal and small: settle your things and find the person expecting you for work.

### C1-01: A Place for Your Things

**Objective:** reach your room, interact with belongings and choose an opening priority. **Gameplay:** movement, camera, object interaction and phone. **Choice:** help Bisi immediately or return after settling in. **Consequence:** a first promise flag, not a morality label. **Recovery:** either order remains valid. **Exit:** player knows where home is and can save.

### C1-02: Collect What You Are Owed

**Objective:** complete an introductory work errand and receive fictional payment. **Gameplay:** travel to Unity Row, speak to Mariam, deliver a package or collect a completed job payment. **Complication:** the contact has stepped out to a community meeting. **Approaches:** wait and converse, follow a known route, or ask for a reliable message. **Consequence:** payment plus different contacts or timing. **Failure:** missed encounter produces a clear return appointment, not permanent loss.

### C1-03: The Way Everybody Uses

**Objective:** accompany Chidi along the route to the bus stop. **Gameplay:** walking, optional conversation and observing the crossing. **Complication:** an awkward delivery needs help getting across. **Choice:** help, suggest an alternative or continue. **Consequence:** learn the route and meet Ngozi. **Exit scene:** a campaign poster promises improvement while a resident says, “Let us see the work.”

### Chapter completion

The player has a home, a source of income and at least two recognisable contacts. A political invitation is visible but optional. The journal distinguishes personal responsibilities from campaign activities. End on a relaxed conversation rather than immediate danger.

## 9. Chapter Two — My Name on the Register

### Narrative purpose

The approaching election becomes a practical task. The player must first find out whether they need registration, completion of a pending process or card collection. A character already holding a card can accompany a neighbour and experience a different version of the chapter.

### C2-01: Check Your Details

**Objective:** establish fictional registration status. **Gameplay:** a simple phone/printing-counter interaction with clearly fictional data. **Branch:** new registration, pending card or existing card. **Complication:** the player confuses a completed online step with having a ready card; Mariam clarifies the next action. **Consequence:** appropriate visit unlocked. **Time:** use an explicit transition where the process needs elapsed story time.

### C2-02: Number Behind Number

**Objective:** join the correct queue at the designated centre. **Gameplay:** read a sign, ask a person, join a slot, converse while progress advances. **Encounters:** a worker worried about a missed shift; someone holding a place for a relative; a helpful person explaining what they were told. **Choice:** assist another person, keep to yourself or challenge a mistaken queue instruction. **Consequence:** relationship information and time trade-offs, with a viable route for quiet players.

The queue should feel long through space, sound, NPC comments and progress—not demand a realistic hour of waiting. Repeat visits can compress already seen interactions.

### C2-03: I Can Help You

**Objective:** respond to Tunde’s unofficial shortcut. **Offer:** fictional cash for supposed quicker access. **Branches:** refuse and wait; ask what service he actually provides; pay; approach the official information point. **Consequences:** money loss or uncertain time advantage on the payment branch; legitimate progress remains possible without payment. An officer can reject the shortcut.

A report branch records an encounter and may produce follow-up, but does not guarantee immediate institutional justice. Never sell a real-money “skip queue” item here.

### C2-04: Your Name, Your Card

**Objective:** complete the scheduled interaction. **Gameplay:** present the appropriate fictional record and answer a short clarification. **Outcomes:** card collected, correction requested, card not ready or already collected status resolved. **Tone:** a small moment of accomplishment after frustration. **Exit:** call or message a familiar person; return to a remaining everyday obligation.

### Chapter completion

The player understands their status and next step. Not being eligible to vote does not remove them from the game or force party membership. All later election scenes check the actual stored story state.

## 10. Chapter Three — The Price of Loyalty

### Narrative purpose

Opportunities become entangled with expectations. The player sees why a person might accept campaign work without being foolish or corrupt. Personal economic pressure supplies the stakes.

### C3-01: An Order Big Enough

**Objective:** help Bisi organise a campaign clothing order or finish a non-campaign customer job. **Complication:** payment terms are vague; somebody assumes her shop will display support. **Choice:** encourage clear terms, accept the ambiguity or decline to get involved. **Consequence:** Bisi’s contract and relationship flags; the player’s party is unchanged.

### C3-02: Come and Hear Us

**Objective:** respond to Efe’s invitation. **Citizen route:** attend, question, listen from outside or decline and complete another task. **Political route:** learn about the group and submit an explicit membership or role application. **Boundary:** attending a meeting is not automatic membership. **Outcome:** Efe remembers how the player engaged, while ordinary work remains accessible.

### C3-03: A Promise on the Wall

**Objective:** help residents collect questions about the crossing and compare a campaign promise with visible work. **Gameplay:** talk to Ngozi, inspect the site, visit a noticeboard. **Choice:** volunteer at a meeting, pass information privately or leave it to others. **Consequence:** a small community record; no omniscient corruption revelation.

### C3-04: Something for Your Time

**Objective:** handle an ambiguous offer connected to campaign attendance or support. **Gameplay:** ask whether the offer is payment for a defined job, reimbursement or an inducement with strings attached. **Choice:** accept legitimate work, refuse an improper condition or accept the questionable offer. **Consequence:** promises and money are recorded separately from vote choice. Secret ballots are never exposed to prove loyalty.

### Chapter completion

The player has accepted, rejected or avoided a consequential opportunity. This provides a relationship problem to revisit later. An independent player receives a complete chapter ending rather than a “join a party to continue” prompt.

## 11. Chapter Four — A Country on the Move

### Narrative purpose

Bring work, transport, community activity and diaspora return together. This is a comparatively active and often humorous chapter before the sustained unease of election eve.

### C4-01: One More Trip

**Passenger version:** board Sanni’s bus to complete an errand. **Driver version:** take a short pre-election shift. **Complication:** a procession causes a detour, a passenger needs the collection centre and Chidi has promised to be somewhere else. **Choices:** wait, take an established alternative, or end the job at a safe agreed stop. **Consequence:** income, timing and reliability; no mandatory political affiliation.

### C4-02: You Have Changed

**Diaspora-player version:** arrive, reconnect with Dara and check where the character is registered. **Local-player version:** help a returning contact navigate changes. **Conflict:** financial expectations and assumptions about life abroad. **Choice:** discuss a boundary, offer specific help or postpone an uncomfortable conversation. **Outcome:** a family relationship changes; the returnee is not treated as automatically wealthy or superior.

### C4-03: Everybody Has an Assignment

**Citizen route:** organise a practical neighbourhood activity, such as clearing a permitted walkway or preparing a community information board. **Political route:** manage a small meeting or outreach assignment. **Shared gameplay:** volunteer roles, supplies, conversations and completion. **Complication:** someone is absent and the plan needs adjustment. **Outcome:** reliability is earned by completing work, not simply selecting a prestigious title.

### C4-04: The Work They Came to Photograph

**Objective:** observe temporary work near the crossing and reconcile competing claims about it. **Choice:** help document what was actually done, challenge an exaggerated description or focus on your own task. **Consequence:** the player may know more about the route’s condition on election day. **Important:** a campaign photograph is not proof that the repair is finished, but it is also not proof the work is fake.

### Chapter completion

A personal commitment remains outstanding. Chidi’s schedule and the route’s condition have been established. These give the coming tension a practical context.

## 12. Chapter Five — Election Eve

### Narrative purpose

Change the atmosphere gradually. Familiar routes, people and sounds carry new uncertainty. The player has choices about preparation, information and whom to meet.

### C5-01: Before They Close

**Objective:** finish one chosen obligation before the neighbourhood winds down. **Options:** collect wages, deliver something promised, obtain ordinary supplies or check on a contact. **Trade-off:** not every optional task can be completed in the same scene window. **Consequence:** missed commitments generate later conversation or an alternate recovery task, not unavoidable death.

### C5-02: Forwarded Many Times

**Objective:** respond to a message about a route or polling change. **Gameplay:** compare its source, ask Mariam, inspect the official in-game notice or speak to a relevant staff contact. **Outcomes:** verified, contradicted or still unknown. **Choice:** share responsibly, repeat as fact or keep quiet. **Consequence:** later trust and who arrives where; no real-world political misinformation is required.

### C5-03: If We Cannot Reach Each Other

**Objective:** agree a meeting point and a fallback plan with Chidi or another established companion. **Gameplay:** choose a reachable place, exchange a message and review the route. **Tone:** an ordinary discussion carrying more worry than either person admits. **Consequence:** a clear reunion option during disruption. **Recovery:** players who skip this receive another understandable lead; the game does not punish ignorance with an automatic fatality.

### C5-04: Last Light on Unity Row

**Objective:** return home or remain at an approved safe location. **Scene:** shutters close, some people joke, a radio report is interrupted by ordinary household activity. Dara calls. **Choice:** reassure, admit worry or discuss a practical plan. **Exit:** save checkpoint and explicit transition to election day.

### Chapter completion

The player knows their intention for tomorrow, the people they may meet and what remains uncertain. No combat encounter is required to make this chapter tense.

## 13. Chapter Six — Election Day Wahala

### 13.1 Shared opening

Begin with a familiar domestic interaction. The player checks their documents/status and receives a short message. The usual street sound is different. Movement and transport reflect the scenario’s researched restrictions; ordinary bus service is not assumed to operate everywhere on election day.

The player may go to vote, accompany someone, pursue an authorised work/story task or remain nearby. Political roles add responsibilities but do not make them immune to civilian concerns.

### C6-01: The Route We Know

**Objective:** reach the school area or chosen alternative destination. **Gameplay:** navigate a known route with changed conditions. **Choice:** use the crossing if it is open, take a marked alternative or ask for an update. **Consequence:** arrival context and encounters, not an arbitrary “bad route = death” rule. **Cue:** residents discuss what they have personally seen versus what they heard.

### C6-02: We Have Been Waiting

**Objective:** understand the polling unit’s current status. **Gameplay:** queue, speak to an appropriate NPC and check a posted explanation. **Possible complication:** delayed materials, a lookup issue, a confusing announcement or no major delay. **Player actions:** wait, assist, seek clarification or leave where allowed. **Consequence:** the story records their presence and knowledge accurately.

### C6-03: A Small Moment of Relief

**Objective:** proceed when a legitimate issue is resolved, or receive a clear next step. **Scene:** a helpful official explains the position; the crowd relaxes slightly; someone resumes a joke. **Function:** give the player genuine relief, not an obvious scripted trick before every attack. In a peaceful variant, this leads into successful voting and a quieter aftermath.

### C6-04: When the Sound Changes

**Default tense-branch trigger:** an authored disturbance near an approach route changes crowd movement. Gunshots may be heard in the selected scenario. The cause is not automatically visible or known. Chidi or the designated companion becomes separated while helping someone move away.

**Objective:** reach a safe navigable area and decide how to respond. **Actions:** follow a readable exit, take cover, contact the companion when feasible, help a nearby person through a limited interaction or seek a staff-provided assembly point. **Design boundary:** the player is not required to fight armed people or run toward gunfire to prove courage.

**Branch outcomes:** the player and companion reunite; the companion is safe elsewhere and contact is delayed; the player arrives at a recovery location; a severe authored variant leads to injury or death in the wider cast. The severity policy must be selected explicitly, not improvised by random damage.

### C6-05: Who Is Still Here?

**Objective:** establish the status of familiar people. **Gameplay:** speak with reachable contacts, check a message or use the agreed meeting point. **Choice:** stay with someone, seek further information or contact family first. **Consequence:** relationship-specific acknowledgement. Do not label a cautious choice as cowardice.

### C6-06: Finish, Leave or Wait

**Objective:** resolve the character’s immediate election-day intention. **Possible results:** voted before the incident, voted after an authorised continuation, unable to vote, chose to leave, or did not intend to vote. **Boundary:** if voting cannot legitimately continue in the scenario, the game should not invent an unofficial ballot workaround.

### 13.2 Scenario variants

| Variant | Dramatic content | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| Peaceful but pressured | Delay, disagreement, eventual clarity | Tension can exist without violence |
| Intimidation | Obstruction and pressure with available exits | Player agency under fear |
| Nearby violent disruption | Gunshots, separation, escape and recovery | Embodied civilian urgency |
| Severe story incident | Fire, injury or death with persistent aftermath | Consequences have weight beyond spectacle |
| Material irregularity | Missing or suspiciously replaced items | Evidence can be incomplete and contested |

These may be separate authored scenarios, not a pile of events occurring at every polling unit. Stage them according to content rating and production decisions. Player account/ballot integrity must not depend on the narrative variant.

### 13.3 Chapter completion

A clear record of ballot status, witnessed events, companion status and unresolved information. End with breathing room: a safe place, a conversation or a quiet journey. Do not immediately overwhelm the player with a scoreboard celebration.

## 14. Chapter Seven — Who Counted My Vote?

### Narrative purpose

The player encounters competing interpretations. Anger and determination replace immediate fear, but personal recovery continues. Election results, local incident evidence and repair accountability remain separate strands.

### C7-01: Three Different Numbers

**Objective:** examine conflicting in-game results messages. **Gameplay:** compare source labels and available records; talk to Mariam or another relevant witness. **Choice:** wait for verified information, share an uncertainty-labelled account or amplify an unsupported claim. **Consequence:** trust and public-information state. **Boundary:** the journal never converts hearsay into an established fact simply because a popular character said it.

### C7-02: What I Actually Saw

**Objective:** preserve or communicate a witness account, if the player chooses. **Gameplay:** select observations the character genuinely experienced, distinguish inference and supply existing evidence. **Alternative:** attend to a personal recovery task instead. **Consequence:** a report can help establish a local timeline without automatically reversing an election.

### C7-03: The Missing Page

**Objective:** follow up on the local repair record. **Revelation:** what was promised, what was delivered and what residents were told do not fully align. A responsible party may explain a delay, acknowledge exaggeration or refuse clarity. **Choices:** pursue a community meeting, help document an agreed next step or disengage. **Boundary:** evidence of a repair dispute does not prove ballot manipulation.

### C7-04: After the Announcement

**Objective:** respond to the declared outcome in the relevant story or online contest. **Political player:** deal with victory, loss or a rules-governed dispute. **Citizen:** visit someone affected, attend a gathering or return home. **Consequence:** the winner changes certain dialogue and governance opportunities, but every player receives a personal scene.

### Chapter completion

The player knows what they established, what remains disputed and which responsibility they will pursue next. The story does not demand certainty about every event to allow an ending.

## 15. Chapter Eight — After the Announcement

### Narrative purpose

Return to ordinary places with changed meaning. The ending should honour both public events and private decisions.

### C8-01: Open the Shutter

**Objective:** help resume an ordinary activity: Bisi’s shop, Mariam’s counter, a driver shift or the player’s own livelihood. **State variations:** a busy reopening, delayed stock, damage cleanup or a quiet conversation with someone recovering. **Choice:** practical assistance, financial help within means or an honest boundary. **Outcome:** a concrete sign of continuity.

### C8-02: The People Still Waiting

**Objective:** attend or decline a community follow-up about the crossing and other local needs. **Gameplay:** revisit an earlier promise, present what was recorded, ask a responsible person for the next step. **Political officeholder:** explain a feasible commitment within their game jurisdiction. **Citizen:** contribute, question or continue another obligation. **Outcome:** repaired, partially improved or unresolved access state; no instant national transformation.

### C8-03: A Conversation You Owe

**Objective:** resolve one major relationship. **Variations:** acknowledge a broken promise, thank someone, apologise for spreading an unverified message, discuss a political disagreement, or sit with grief. **Outcome:** reconciliation, respectful distance or an unresolved but honestly expressed boundary. Buying an item does not automatically repair trust.

### C8-04: The Road Home

**Objective:** choose the next personal direction. **Choices:** remain and build the livelihood, continue community work, pursue political responsibility, return abroad or leave for another opportunity. **Final movement:** walk or travel through the familiar route. Signs of the player’s history appear naturally in the environment and conversations.

### Final scene principle

The closing image depends on lived choices: a reopened shop, an answered message, a companion beside you, a road still needing work or an empty place where someone used to sit. A national winner announcement can be heard in the background, but it does not erase the personal ending.

## 16. Optional political-career story paths

### 16.1 Group member

Entry: explicit application or membership action. First task: show up to a meeting and take a defined volunteer responsibility. Conflict: a group request competes with an ordinary obligation. Possible ending: remain a member, disagree internally or resign while retaining civilian progress.

### 16.2 Campaign manager

Entry: apply and receive authorised appointment. Missions: allocate a small event budget, assign volunteers, resolve an absence and communicate a changed plan. Human conflict: leadership wants visible attendance while transport conditions or family commitments complicate participation.

The strongest dramatic decision is not simply “honest or corrupt.” It can be whether to admit an event is not ready, disappoint a candidate, or ask people to take on more than they agreed. The manager cannot inspect anyone’s ballot or confiscate personal money.

### 16.3 House of Representatives candidate

Entry follows the final constituency/candidacy rules. Missions: listen to local concerns, make a scoped commitment, choose a campaign team, answer a difficult question and respond to results. Jurisdiction must be researched before promising particular powers. A candidate’s personal relationships and livelihood do not disappear.

### 16.4 Presidential candidate

Player candidates occupy the limited slots according to the resolved first-ten policy. They build teams, debate, communicate plans and campaign across represented regions as content expands. The local storyline uses flexible references to the player’s organisation and result rather than hard-coding one named NPC as inevitable president.

This route is not implemented fully in the first neighbourhood slice. A candidate should have interesting responsibilities without becoming the only character whose choices matter.

### 16.5 Independent citizen parity

For every political chapter objective, supply a citizen objective of comparable narrative importance: earn income, help a neighbour, make a travel decision, document an experience or resolve a personal conflict. Independence is an active life path, not a missing menu option.

## 17. Livelihoods, danfo journeys and side stories

| Side story | Opening | Complication | Meaningful outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your Change Is Coming | A familiar bus fare conversation | The conductor is distracted and the player needs the money | Resolve it respectfully, leave a debt reminder or damage trust |
| One More Trip | A driving shift near campaign activity | Detour and competing passenger needs | Income plus a reliability consequence |
| Before Match Starts | Friends gather at a viewing centre | Politics threatens to take over the evening | Set a boundary, engage or leave; preserve ordinary recreation |
| The Cloth Is Not Free | Bisi receives a large order | Payment and endorsement expectations blur | Clear contract, delayed payment or strained relationship |
| Send Me the Location | A returning relative needs help | Their expectations do not match current conditions | Honest reconnection without treating diaspora as a stereotype |
| The Notice on the Wall | A community announcement is unclear | People repeat different interpretations | Improve information and see fewer misunderstandings |
| Someone Has to Open | An aftermath business task | The owner is exhausted or recovering | A practical act of solidarity or a respected refusal |

Side missions should introduce a person or condition that can return later. Do not pad the map with interchangeable errands. Repeatable work can be mechanically simpler, but its earnings, travel and social context should remain consistent.

## 18. Dialogue and cinematic scenes

These are sample lines to establish tone. They require Nigerian dialogue review and eventual localisation. Pidgin is used selectively; not every character speaks the same way.

### 18.1 Opening at the compound

**Bisi:** “You’re the person moving into that room?”  
**Player:** “That depends. Is there something I should know?”  
**Bisi:** “Only that this package is blocking your door. Help me with one side.”  
**Chidi, passing:** “You never unpack, work don start.”  
**Bisi:** “And you that have unpacked since last year—come and carry something.”

Camera stays playable. Helping is an interaction, not a cutscene that automatically establishes the player as generous.

### 18.2 At the queue

**Tunde:** “You want to finish today? I can help you.”  
**Player option A:** “Help me understand where I’m supposed to stand.”  
**Tunde:** “That one is different.”  
**Player option B:** “I’ll wait.”  
**Tunde:** “Your choice.”  
**Mrs. Akinola, later:** “Let me check the record first. If there’s a problem, I’ll tell you what the next step is.”

The interface presents a fictional monetary choice only where relevant. It never implies the official fee is the unofficial demand.

### 18.3 Campaign invitation

**Efe:** “You know people listen when you explain something. We need that.”  
**Player:** “Do you need me to help, or do you need me to agree?”  
**Efe:** “I’m asking you to come and hear us first.”

Options: attend, ask about a role, ask a policy question, or decline. The membership state changes only through an explicit subsequent action.

### 18.4 Election eve

**Dara:** “You’re answering as if I’m disturbing you.”  
**Player:** “I’m trying to finish something.”  
**Dara:** “Finish it. Then tell me when you get home.”  
**Player option:** “I will.”  
**Dara:** “You said that yesterday.”

If yesterday’s promise was kept, substitute a different line. Dialogue must respect the actual save state.

### 18.5 Disturbance and recovery

The crowd sound changes before the camera finds a cause. An NPC looks toward the gate. Someone calls for space. A directional sound occurs in the chosen scenario. The player retains control and can see an exit.

**Chidi:** “This side—there’s space here.”  
After separation and a safe regrouping:  
**Player:** “I thought you were behind me.”  
**Chidi:** “I was. Then everybody moved.”

Leave a short silence. Avoid an immediate joke that erases the emotional beat. Later humour can signal genuine recovery.

### 18.6 Aftermath

**Bisi:** “They’ve announced the result.”  
**Player:** “I heard.”  
**Bisi:** “Help me lift this shutter.”

The action returns the story to ordinary work. Follow-up dialogue changes with damage, payment, relationship and election state. This scene should not imply civic outcomes are meaningless; it shows that life continues alongside them.

## 19. Branching, continuity and multiplayer rules

### 19.1 Narrative state groups

- **Personal:** origin, priority, home, livelihood, registration/card state, completed promises and ballot status.
- **Relationships:** specific help, disagreements, debts, trust events and reconciliation.
- **Knowledge:** witnessed fact, sourced report, rumour, correction and unresolved claim.
- **World:** chapter/season phase, route condition, site availability, public result and community project state.
- **Incidents:** scenario, exposure, injury/recovery, companion location and verified loss.
- **Political:** organisation, appointment, candidacy, office term and permissions.

Example flags: `kept_bisi_delivery_promise`, `card_collected`, `queue_shortcut_paid`, `agreed_meeting_point`, `rumour_corrected`, `companion_reunited`, `crossing_status`. Store stable identifiers rather than using dialogue text as keys.

### 19.2 Branch budget

Each mission should have a shared spine, a small number of meaningful alternatives and a reconnection point. Consequences can alter later dialogue, route access, rewards or an aftermath scene without requiring eight completely separate campaigns.

Do not make every choice fatal. Major losses require authored setup and review. Track what the player could reasonably know when deciding. If an outcome feels unfair, improve the cue or choice rather than explaining it only in a later text box.

### 19.3 Multiplayer conflict policy — proposed

Personal conversations and origin stories can be instanced. Shared elections and world phases are authoritative. A player cannot rewind the community’s election by replaying a chapter. Completed personal scenes can be replayed in a clearly separated story mode without awarding duplicate online rewards.

For severe NPC outcomes, use one of two explicit approaches: a shared server-wide authored event, or a private story instance with private aftermath. Do not let the same shared NPC be dead for one player and actively serving another in the same physical scene without an intentional explanation. Decide the scope when authoring the mission.

Candidate dialogue uses role placeholders and actual current contest state. No script forces a named character to win an online election. The player’s eyewitness narrative and the competitive ballot ledger remain separate systems.

### 19.4 Absence, late arrival and recovery

A disconnected player re-enters at a safe checkpoint with committed outcomes preserved. Missed real-time events can be recapped through a journal or personal scene, but the game must not invent participation or cast a vote for them. A late-arriving citizen receives a viable personal story even if candidacy slots are full.

## 20. Endings and epilogues

### 20.1 Ending composition

Build the ending from four components: personal direction, relationship outcomes, community condition and political result. Avoid one “good ending” that requires completing every task or winning office.

| Ending family | Conditions / emphasis | Closing image |
|---|---|---|
| A Life Taking Shape | Stable livelihood and a chosen local future | Player opens for work or returns home with purpose |
| The People Who Stayed | Strong relationships despite incomplete public progress | A shared task and a familiar conversation |
| A Public Promise | Player holds office or responsibility | A concrete commitment followed by citizen questions |
| Home, Then Away | Diaspora return or departure path | Leaving with changed relationships and ongoing contact |
| An Unfinished Road | Community problem remains unresolved | A usable next step rather than false total closure |
| The Place We Keep | Severe loss in the chosen scenario | A respectful absence, memory or recovery activity |
| A Different Kind of Distance | Broken trust remains unrepaired | Familiar location visited without the old intimacy |

These can combine. A winning candidate can lose a friendship. An independent driver can finish with strong community trust. A peaceful playthrough can still end with disappointment about promises.

### 20.2 Epilogue cards

Use short scene-backed statements instead of a lecture: Bisi’s order was paid late but her shop remained independent; the crossing received temporary work and residents continued asking for completion; Mariam corrected a report and changed how her channel labels sources; Chidi kept the next promise he made.

Only state outcomes established by gameplay. Do not claim the player single-handedly fixed a national institution or that one shared item changed an election result unless the system actually supports it.

### 20.3 Next-season hooks

An unresolved community promise, a new work opportunity, a relationship in another state, or a diaspora invitation can lead into future content. The first season must still have emotional closure. Do not withhold every resolution to sell an expansion.

## 21. Art, sound and cultural storytelling

Akinwale needs locally grounded references: clothing worn for ordinary work, buses and stops, small-business interiors, public-school details, street surfaces and domestic spaces. Maintain variation in income and building condition. Nigerian identity should not be reduced to yellow buses, dust and danger.

Voice direction: people speak to one another, not to a tourist tutorial. Explain unfamiliar terms through context or optional glossary. Use English, Pidgin and other languages according to character and setting; exact language scope remains open. Obtain native-speaker review rather than relying on imitation.

The emotional sound palette shifts: ordinary layered activity, thinner ambience, indistinct distant activity, specific incident cues, then recovery sounds. Subtitle critical information and keep effects adjustable. Silence can be meaningful, but it should not remove necessary navigation cues.

Cinematic ambition is compatible with a modest prototype: strong staging, readable faces, good timing and consistent sound can precede expensive graphics. The long-term PlayStation-like aspiration does not require a console build to test whether the story affects players.

## 22. Mission-writing contract and production priorities

### 22.1 Required mission record

Every production mission expands this treatment into: ID; title; chapter; location; prerequisites; participating characters; player objective; interaction steps; dialogue graph; optional branches; state changes; failure and recovery; checkpoint; world/instance scope; assets; audio; research needs; acceptance tests; next mission.

### 22.2 Recommended first story slice

Build **C1-01 → a shortened C1-02 → C2-01 → C2-02 → C2-03 → C2-04**, followed by a brief home conversation. Use a temporary cast and simple environments until interaction/save systems work. Include the honest route and refusal of political recruitment. A passenger journey can be its first extension.

This proves the citizen-first experience without requiring the entire campaign. The first separate tension test should use familiar characters from that slice, then a short election-day warning, separation/reunion and aftermath. Do not stage severe death content before the team can deliver ordinary attachment and readable escape.

### 22.3 Narrative acceptance criteria

A new player can explain their personal goal; recognises at least two people; can finish independently of a party; knows whether information is verified; understands card status; sees a consequence of a choice; notices a change in mood; can identify an available response to danger; and reaches an ending that reflects their actual actions.

Test dialogue continuity after accepting/declining, paying/refusing, missing/keeping a promise, reconnecting and loading an older save. Test a peaceful playthrough as carefully as a violent one. Ask reviewers where a character sounds false or a situation feels generalised.

### 22.4 Writing production order

1. Approve or revise this central plot and recurring cast.
2. Fully script the first citizen slice, including alternate and recovery lines.
3. Playtest it with temporary visuals and revise pacing.
4. Write the first danfo journey and livelihood extension.
5. Script the election-eve and one election-day scenario, with clear incident scope.
6. Write aftermath variants before expanding the incident catalogue.
7. Add political-role branches and connect them to verified multiplayer rules.
8. Build additional origin and regional stories through locally reviewed briefs.
