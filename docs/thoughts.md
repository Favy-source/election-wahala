For **your presidential-ticket idea**, imagine this:

- Citizens play and vote for free.
- A player pays **₦2,000** to contest a particular election season.
- A room has ten candidate slots.
- A full room produces **₦20,000 in gross ticket sales**.

Those are illustrative prices. With 100 fully subscribed rooms, that would be ₦2 million gross per season—but it requires **1,000 paying candidates plus enough active citizens across those rooms**. Finding and retaining those players is the difficult part.

There are also practical questions: what happens if someone pays and the room never fills, the season is cancelled, or a technical problem prevents them from campaigning? We would need clear reservation and refund rules. We should only charge once their candidate slot is secured.

**My preference is free candidacy in public elections, with optional purchases around the experience.** Here is the model I would test:

| Revenue stream | What people buy | Why it fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Character cosmetics** | Outfits, hairstyles, accessories and emotes | Citizens and candidates can both buy them |
| **Campaign customisation** | Poster designs, clothing and rally decorations | Gives candidates personality without buying extra votes |
| **Private election rooms** | A hosted season for friends, creators or communities | One organiser pays; invited players participate |
| **Extra story chapters** | Additional diaspora journeys, careers and family stories | Monetises the storytelling you care about |
| **Brand sponsorships** | Clearly labelled shop signs, billboards and sponsored events | Makes use of the Nigerian world we are building |
| **Supporter pack** | A fixed bundle of cosmetics and a supporter badge | Gives early fans a straightforward way to support development |

For campaign customisation, everyone should still have the same basic opportunities to reach voters. Paid items should not secretly increase NPC support, improve ballot weight or guarantee victory.

**Private rooms are the strongest alternative to charging each presidential aspirant.** For example, a creator could pay to host “My Community’s Election Weekend,” invite their audience and let ten people contest. That gives the buyer a concrete service while keeping participation accessible.

I’d also keep advertisements out of moments such as fleeing a disturbance or finding an injured relative. The tension you want would suffer if a full-screen advert interrupted it. Sponsored street scenery and placements between chapters fit better.
